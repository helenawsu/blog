A goldfish is freshly made in a factory and celebrates with its peers. They are all the original cheddar flavor. However, they notice there are other fancier and more colorful flavors on the other assembly lines—the Rainbow Veggie, the Double Cheddar, and the Pretzels.

The original flavor goldfish becomes sad because it feels it is not as cool, attractive, or delicious as the others. Some of the nearby fish share this sentiment; they want to become the other flavors. It studies the assembly lines and notices the fish are born with their flavors, and ahead is only packaging. No spicing or color can be altered once they are made.

It thinks of a new idea. Instead of becoming another flavor since it can’t be changed, it can jump to the lines where the fancy flavors are packaged so it can be mixed in. Its friends agree. Some fish jump; none of them succeed. Some drop to the ground and shatter.

It decides it will accept its flavor and its packaging. It is transported to a supermarket and lined up with all the other flavors on the shelf, watching people come and go. To its surprise, the classic cheddar flavor is still the most popular. It is soon bought before all the other flavors. It is happy and feels lucky it didn’t try changing its flavor or jumping to another line.

However, when it is packed into a lunch box and eaten—shattered by a child—it realizes it is no different from the jumping fish that shattered on the factory floor.
